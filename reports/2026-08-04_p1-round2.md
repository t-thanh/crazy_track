## Part A

### A.1 The variance lesson now rests on ten seeds — and the three-seed version was wrong in both directions

Poolability was checked before anything was reused. Seeds 0–2 live under
`ms-lhfix-*` at git `0b1668b`; the only file touched since that affects a
rollout is `rollout.py`, and only by the additive `meas_pos` logging. A re-run
of seed 0 at the current code state (`p1-mppi-lhfast-verify-s0`) returned
**0.480325 against the stored 0.480325** — bit-identical — so seeds 0–2 pool
with the new 3–9.

Per-seed (LH, fast tier, tuned MPPI):

```
0.480  0.091  0.114  0.130  0.108  0.093  0.125  0.104  0.218  0.107
```

| | n | mean | sd | min | max | worst/best |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| old (seeds 0–2) | 3 | 0.228 | 0.178 | 0.091 | 0.480 | 5.3× |
| **new** | 10 | **0.157** | **0.113** | 0.091 | 0.480 | 5.3× |

**Stated plainly, as requested: the ten-seed spread is narrower than the
three-seed one, and the mean is lower by a third.** The three-seed sample
gave the 0.480 outlier a third of the weight instead of a tenth, inflating
both statistics. What survives untouched is the qualitative claim: the
worst draw is still 5.3× the best, one seed in ten still lands at 0.480 and
another at 0.218, and eight of ten sit below 0.130 — a skewed distribution
for which a mean is a poor summary. The lesson is unchanged; the numbers
quoted for it were not defensible and now are.

This is the second time in two rounds that our own small sample misled us
(round 1: ADRC's single seed was the minimum of ten). Lesson 3 now records
this instance in the paper, because a lesson about small samples is more
credible when it is applied to the paper's own numbers.
