\begin{abstract}
Trajectory-tracking controllers for nano-quadrotors are typically reported on
different platforms, references, metrics and sensing stacks, so their relative merits
--- and, more importantly, their failure modes --- are difficult to compare. We
benchmark eight controller stacks on one simulated platform (Crazyflie~2.1 brushless,
$43.4$~g, thrust-to-weight $1.88$), one reference family (figure-eight Lissajous at
three cycle times) and one command interface: a PID with acceleration feedforward, an
active disturbance rejection controller (ADRC) with a reduced-order velocity extended
state observer, sampling-based MPPI with $\mathcal{L}_1$ adaptation, nonlinear MPC on a
system-identified attitude model, an offset-free MPC variant, two classical outer loops
over a pretrained learned adaptive rate controller, and a DATT-style learned policy in
three variants ($\mathcal{L}_1$-augmented, noise-trained, and privileged-critic).
Each stack is evaluated under constant wind, gusts, in-ground effect, added payload, and
a Lighthouse measurement model grounded in published characterization data
($34\pm18$~Hz zero-order-hold position, $2$--$4$~cm quasi-static bias), using ten
evaluation seeds and three training seeds so that claims are statistically qualified
rather than single-run. No stack dominates. Under the realistic deployment condition
(Lighthouse sensing with wind) three unrelated architectures converge to statistically
indistinguishable errors of $0.057\pm0.014$, $0.059\pm0.002$ and $0.060\pm0.009$~m,
which we argue makes that regime estimator-limited rather than architecture-limited.
Every failure we observe falls into one of three classes: missing model structure,
estimator bandwidth-versus-noise trade-offs, and information bottlenecks in memoryless
learned policies. We further show that mean-only reporting misleads --- two pool leaders
fail only on unlucky sensor-bias draws, and an apparent noise fragility of offset-free
MPC is a launch transient, removed by soft-starting the observer and shown to be generic
to the MPC family. All results are in simulation; absolute errors are optimistic
relative to flight, and we report relative comparisons together with the evidence trail
for every claim.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{quadrotor control, trajectory tracking, benchmarking, disturbance rejection,
