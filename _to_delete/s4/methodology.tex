\section{METHODOLOGY}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Platform and simulator}
\label{sec:method:platform}

All experiments use the Crazyflie~2.1 Brushless. It has a mass of
\SI{43.38}{\gram} and an inertia of
$\operatorname{diag}(25, 28, 49)\times10^{-6}$~\si{\kilogram\meter\squared}, and its
four motors deliver $0.0214$--\SI{0.2}{\newton} each, giving a collective thrust of
$0.085$--\SI{0.8}{\newton} and a thrust-to-weight ratio of $1.88$. The narrowness of
that margin is the defining property of the platform: a \SI{10}{\gram} payload is
\SI{23}{\percent} of vehicle weight, and a \SI{2.5}{\meter\per\second\squared} wind
consumes roughly a quarter of the available lateral acceleration.

Simulation is performed in \texttt{crazyflow}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/utiasDSL/crazyflow}} The ground-truth plant is
a first-principles rigid-body model integrated at \SI{500}{\hertz} with a
firmware-replica Mellinger attitude loop, so that every controller in the pool acts
through the same inner loop the physical vehicle uses. The two predictive controllers
(Sec.~\ref{sec:method:pool}) use a different, deliberately mismatched internal model: a
system-identified second-order closed-loop attitude model with symbolic export for
optimization. We keep this mismatch rather than granting the optimizers the true model,
because a predictive controller in deployment never has the true model, and the
consequences of that gap are among the findings of this study.

The simulator omits aerodynamic drag, motor asymmetry, battery sag, and --- unless the
measurement model of Sec.~\ref{sec:method:sensor} is enabled --- state-estimation noise
and latency. Absolute errors are therefore optimistic; we compared our nominal figures
against published real-world results on comparable vehicles and found them roughly five
to ten times better, entirely accounted for by those omissions. Consequently this paper
claims \emph{relative} comparisons under shared conditions, not absolute performance,
and the hardware campaign of Sec.~\ref{sec:method:hardware} exists precisely to test
whether the relative ordering survives.

\subsection{Reference family, metrics, and fairness protocol}
\label{sec:method:metrics}

The reference is the figure-eight Lissajous used as a tracking benchmark in the
learned-control literature\cite{eschmann2024learning},
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{r}(t) = \big[\, A\cos\omega t,\;\; B\sin 2\omega t,\;\; z \,\big]^\top,
  \qquad \omega = \frac{2\pi}{T},
  \label{eq:lissajous}
\end{equation}
with $A = \SI{1}{\meter}$, $B = \SI{0.5}{\meter}$, $z = \SI{1}{\meter}$, flown for two
complete cycles at three cycle times: $T \in \{15.0,\, 5.5,\, 3.5\}$~\si{\second},
referred to as \emph{slow}, \emph{normal} and \emph{fast}. The fast tier reaches
approximately \SI{3}{\meter\per\second} and \SI{0.9}{\g} of lateral acceleration, close
to the platform's envelope. For the ground-effect condition the trajectory is lowered to
$z = \SI{0.08}{\meter}$.

Tracking accuracy is reported as the root-mean-square of the three-dimensional position
error and, where the vertical channel matters, of its horizontal component,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{3D}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}}
  \lVert \mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{r}_i \rVert_2^2},
  \qquad
  \mathcal{I} = \{\,i : t_i \ge \SI{1}{\second}\,\},
  \label{eq:rmse}
\end{equation}
together with the maximum error over the same window. The vehicle starts at rest at
$\mathbf{r}(0)$ while the reference already carries a nonzero velocity, so the first
second is excluded as a warm-up. That exclusion is a convention inherited from the
benchmark we replicate; Sec.~\ref{sec:results:lessons} shows it is not always
sufficient, and that treating it as sufficient produced a wrong conclusion.

Fairness is enforced at the command interface. Every controller emits
$[\phi,\theta,\psi,f]$ --- roll, pitch, yaw and collective thrust --- at
\SI{100}{\hertz}, with yaw commanded to zero throughout, and every controller receives
the same state vector from the same source. The single deviation is the hybrid adaptive
stack, whose pretrained low-level controller was trained at \SI{500}{\hertz} and
therefore runs its rate loop at that rate, with its outer position loop decimated to
\SI{100}{\hertz} to match the rest of the pool. Running that outer loop at
\SI{500}{\hertz} on per-sample sensor noise was measured to be catastrophic, which is
itself informative: the decimation is not a concession but the correct design.

\subsection{Controller pool}
\label{sec:method:pool}

The pool contains one representative per control paradigm, each selected as the best
member of its own family in our data. Table~\ref{tab:pool} summarizes the resulting
configurations.

\paragraph{PID with acceleration feedforward.}
A cascaded position loop with full reference feedforward, $k_p = 16$, $k_d = 8$
(critically damped at \SI{4}{\radian\per\second}), $k_i = 2$, and the integral state
saturated at \SI{0.5}{\meter\second}. Desired acceleration becomes an attitude and
thrust command through the differential-flatness allocation shared by all attitude-mode
stacks\cite{mellinger2011minimum}. This is an honest baseline rather than a strawman: it
has full feedforward, and the saturated integrator is exactly the mechanism that limits
it under sustained loads.

\paragraph{Active disturbance rejection control.}
A reduced-order extended state observer on measured velocity estimates a lumped
disturbance acceleration $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, in the standard bandwidth
parameterization\cite{gao2003scaling,han2009adrc}. That estimate is saturated at
\SI{3}{\meter\per\second\squared} and subtracted from the control law in place of an
integrator. The single tuning knob is the observer bandwidth $\omega_o$, set here to
\SI{7}{\radian\per\second}; how that value was reached, and why no value is correct, is
Lesson~2 in Sec.~\ref{sec:results:lessons}.

\paragraph{Sampling-based predictive control.}
Model-predictive path-integral control\cite{williams2018mppi} rolls $N = 512$ input
sequences over $H = 25$ steps at \SI{0.02}{\second} through the identified attitude
model and applies the exponentially weighted mean, $\lambda = 0.02$. Exploration noise
is temporally correlated, AR(1) with $\beta = 0.7$; replacing white noise with it was
the single largest tuning gain we measured, because white per-step perturbations average
themselves out over a \SI{0.5}{\second} horizon. An $\mathcal{L}_1$ adaptive
estimator\cite{hovakimyan2010l1} supplies a disturbance acceleration held constant
across the rollout horizon.

\paragraph{Offset-free nonlinear MPC.}
A multiple-shooting nonlinear program over the same identified model, $H = 20$ at
\SI{0.04}{\second}, penalizing position error, velocity-reference error, input magnitude
and input rate, solved by an interior-point method with warm starting. The model is
augmented with a constant disturbance-acceleration state supplied by the same velocity
observer, the standard offset-free construction\cite{maeder2009offsetfree}. One addition
proved necessary: the estimate fed to the optimizer is ramped in over the first
\SI{1.5}{\second} of flight,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{d}_{\mathrm{plan}}(t) = \boldsymbol{\sigma}(t)\cdot\min\!\left(1,\ \frac{t}{1.5}\right),
  \label{eq:softstart}
\end{equation}
approximately three observer convergence times. The mechanism exists because a cold
observer's first innovations are noise-dominated at full gain, and an optimizer that
plans against that estimate acts on a disturbance that is not there. What it corrected,
and how we first misread it, is Lesson~4.

\paragraph{Hybrid adaptive stack.}
An ADRC outer loop as above, commanding body rates into a \emph{pretrained} learned
low-level controller\cite{zhang2025xadapt} that maps rate commands to motor speeds and
adapts online from a history of recent states and commands. The low-level network has
never seen this airframe; the only calibration performed was the maximum motor speed,
from a hover sweep.

\paragraph{Reinforcement-learning policy.}
A DATT-style tracking policy\cite{huang2023datt} trained with
PPO\cite{schulman2017ppo}, described in Sec.~\ref{sec:method:policy}.

\subsection{The learned policy}
\label{sec:method:policy}

The policy observes position error, velocity, attitude quaternion, a ten-step relative
preview of the reference spanning \SI{0.6}{\second} ahead, and an onboard
$\mathcal{L}_1$ disturbance-acceleration estimate --- $43$ values --- and emits roll,
pitch and collective thrust, roll and pitch scaled to $\pm\SI{0.7}{\radian}$. PPO,
\SI{50}{\hertz} decisions, \SI{6}{\second} episodes, sixteen parallel worlds, two layers
of $64$ units, reward $\exp(-2\lVert\mathbf{e}\rVert) - 0.02\lVert\mathbf{a}\rVert$ with
a terminal penalty on crashing or exceeding \SI{2}{\meter} of error.

Training references are random $C^2$ chained quintics with per-episode difficulty (knot
velocities $\mathcal{U}(0.5, 3.5)$~\si{\meter\per\second}, accelerations
$\mathcal{U}(1, 10)$~\si{\meter\per\second\squared}), so the policy sees a distribution
of aggressiveness rather than the benchmark itself. \textbf{The Lissajous is never seen
during training; all reported policy results are zero-shot.} Domain randomization covers
a per-episode constant force of up to \SI{3.5}{\meter\per\second\squared} per axis
(halved vertically), \SI{\pm5}{\centi\meter} of initial scatter, and a per-episode
sensor-noise scale (Sec.~\ref{sec:method:sensor}) from $\mathcal{U}(0, 1.5)$.

The benchmark variant uses an asymmetric actor--critic\cite{pinto2018asymmetric}: the
actor sees only the noisy, delayed observation above, the critic additionally the true
error, velocity, attitude and perturbation; the privileged block is discarded at
deployment. Two earlier variants appear in Sec.~\ref{sec:results:lessons} as evidence
only: one with the $\mathcal{L}_1$ channel and force randomization but clean
observations, one additionally trained on noisy observations without the privileged
critic.\footnote{Repository tags: DATT-L1~$=$~v3, DATT-Noisy~$=$~v4,
DATT-Asym~$=$~v5, DATT-Stack~$=$~v6a.}

\subsection{Disturbance models}
\label{sec:method:disturbance}

Four disturbances are injected as world-frame forces on the center of mass, chosen so
that each isolates a different failure mechanism and each is reproducible as a physical
test condition (Sec.~\ref{sec:method:hardware}).

\emph{Constant wind} applies a steady \SI{0.11}{\newton}
($\approx\SI{2.5}{\meter\per\second\squared}$) lateral force --- a standing load that
any integrating or observing controller should eventually cancel, and that a saturated
integrator cannot. \emph{Gusting wind} superimposes on a \SI{0.08}{\newton} mean a
\SI{0.7}{\hertz} sinusoidal component and an Ornstein--Uhlenbeck turbulence process
($\sigma = 0.04$, $\tau = \SI{0.5}{\second}$), probing estimator \emph{bandwidth} rather
than steady-state accuracy. \emph{Payload} adds \SI{10}{\gram} (\SI{23}{\percent} of
weight) as a constant downward force, an airframe change rather than an environmental
one. \emph{Ground effect} applies the Cheeseman--Bennett thrust
gain\cite{cheeseman1955ige} $1/\big(1 - (R/4z)^2\big)$ on a low-altitude trajectory,
a multiplicative actuation disturbance rather than an additive force. The magnitudes
bracket the perturbation range used in the learned-tracking
literature\cite{huang2023datt} and sit within the envelope of the wind facility.

\subsection{Realistic sensing: the Lighthouse measurement model}
\label{sec:method:sensor}

Clean state feedback flatters every controller equally and hides the differences that
matter in deployment, so all conditions are additionally run through a measurement model
of the lighthouse-style optical positioning system the vehicle actually carries,
parameterized from a published characterization
dataset\cite{taffanel2021lighthouse}. Position arrives as a zero-order hold updated at
$34\pm18$~\si{\hertz}, carrying \SI{0.7}{\milli\meter} of jitter and a quasi-static
per-episode spatial bias of \SI{1.5}{\centi\meter} per axis
($\approx\SI{2.6}{\centi\meter}$ in three dimensions); velocity and attitude, fused
onboard from inertial measurements, carry \SI{3}{\centi\meter\per\second} and
\SI{0.5}{\degree} of noise; the rate gyroscope carries
\SI{0.02}{\radian\per\second}. One control step (\SI{10}{\milli\second}) of latency is
applied to the optical chain only --- the gyroscope is an onboard inertial sensor with
no transport delay, and delaying it destabilizes any high-rate inner loop, a modelling
error we made and corrected during this study.

Two properties of this model drive the results that follow. The bias is drawn once per
episode and held, so it behaves like an unknown constant offset rather than averaging
away --- which is why evaluation seeds matter and why some controllers fail only on
particular draws. And the reported \SI{2}{}--\SI{4}{\centi\meter} accuracy figure is
itself referenced to motion capture, which makes it directly testable by the hardware
setup described next.

\subsection{Statistical protocol}
\label{sec:method:stats}

Two independent sources of randomness are separated rather than pooled. \emph{Evaluation
seeds} drive the sensor bias draw, the turbulence realization and the sampling noise of
MPPI; every cell involving a stochastic condition is run at ten evaluation seeds.
\emph{Training seeds} drive policy initialization; every policy-level claim is run at
three training seeds with the evaluation seed held fixed, so that training variance is
not confounded with evaluation variance. Cells are reported as mean $\pm$ standard
deviation, with the range in brackets where the spread rather than the mean is the
finding. Every run directory carries the date, a mandatory free-text reason, the source
commit and the full configuration, and each claim in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} is traceable
to its runs.
