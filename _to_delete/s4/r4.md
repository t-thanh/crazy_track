### E.3 Still weakly evidenced or over-claimed — fresh-eyes pass

Four, in descending order of how much they would cost if a reviewer pressed:

1. **"Each the best member of its own family in our data" is doing unexamined work.**
   It appears in the abstract, the contributions, the methodology and the conclusion, and
   it is what licenses "one representative per paradigm". For ADRC and the offset-free
   MPC it is well supported (documented sweeps). For MPPI it rests on a single tuning
   sweep of four configurations, and for the PID baseline on no sweep at all — its gains
   are stated but never shown to be the best of its family. The claim is strongest where
   the paper spent effort and weakest where it did not, which is precisely the asymmetry
   the paper criticises in others (§1, "most tune the method under study carefully and
   its alternatives casually"). Either soften to "a competently tuned representative", or
   say per stack how its configuration was chosen.
2. **The four-way tie is a tie of two different statistical objects.** Three members are
   ten *evaluation* seeds; DATT-Asym is three *training* seeds, which is a different
   source of variance measured a different way. The paper is scrupulous about labelling
   them, but "statistically indistinguishable" glosses that they are not commensurable —
   an evaluation-seed spread and a training-seed spread do not combine into one
   comparison without an argument the paper does not make.
3. **"Estimator-limited, not architecture-limited" is an interpretation presented as a
   finding.** The evidence is four stacks saturating at ~0.06 m. That is consistent with
   an estimator bound, but also with four stacks all being limited by the same *actuator*
   envelope, or by the reference itself at that tier. The paper never tests the
   estimator hypothesis directly — for instance by re-running the deployment cell with
   the sensor model's bias halved and showing all four improve together. One such run
   would convert the strongest claim in the paper from inference to demonstration, and
   it is cheap.
4. **The recommendation table's compute column invites a misreading.** It reports
   whole-episode wall-clock in an unoptimised CPU harness, ~3 s of which is simulation
   overhead common to every row. The footnote says so, but the column is headed "Cost"
   and will be read as controller cost; the optimizer's 41 s against the baseline's 3.1 s
   is a ratio of harness episodes, not of control loops.

Items 1 and 2 are honesty-of-framing; item 3 is the one worth an experiment.
